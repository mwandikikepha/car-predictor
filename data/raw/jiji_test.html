Cars in Kenya: Complete Buyer's Guide (2025)

Looking to buy a car in Kenya? With over 90,000 vehicles imported annually and local prices ranging from KSh 500,000 to KSh 15 million, finding the right car requires expert knowledge. This comprehensive guide covers everything from popular models to financing options, helping you make an informed purchase decision.

Popular Car Brands in Kenya

Kenya's automobile market is dominated by Japanese brands, according to data from the Kenya Motor Industry Association. Toyota leads with approximately 27% market share, followed by Nissan (18%), Mitsubishi (12%), and Mercedes-Benz and Ford account for smaller percentages.

James Mutua, automotive expert at AutoXpress Kenya, explains: "Japanese cars dominate our market due to their reliability, fuel efficiency, and availability of spare parts. Their affordability and ability to handle Kenyan roads make them particularly attractive to local buyers."

New vs. Used Cars: What Kenyans Prefer

Over 85% of cars purchased in Kenya are used vehicles, primarily imported from Japan, the UK, and UAE. A survey conducted among 500 Kenyan car owners revealed that 78% preferred used imports due to significant cost savings.

David Kiarie, a satisfied Jiji user who purchased a Toyota Fielder last year, shares: "I saved nearly KSh 1.2 million by buying a well-maintained three-year-old import rather than a new model. The condition was excellent, and I've had no mechanical issues so far."

Used Car Buying Tips

  • Check the mileage - Verify it matches the car's condition and age
  • Inspect for rust - Especially underneath and around wheel wells
  • Research import year - Kenya restricts imports older than 8 years
  • Verify chassis number - Ensure it matches documentation
  • Test all electronics - AC, power windows, and entertainment systems

Car Financing Options in Kenya

With average vehicle prices exceeding KSh 1.5 million, financing is essential for many buyers. Currently, banks offer auto loans at interest rates between 12-18%, while SACCOs typically provide slightly lower rates at 10-14%.

Catherine Wanjiku, financial advisor at Equity Bank, advises: "Most Kenyans qualify for 70-80% financing with repayment periods between 3-5 years. A good credit score can significantly improve your interest rate, potentially saving hundreds of thousands over the loan term."

Pros and Cons of Buying Cars in Kenya

Advantages

  • Investment potential - Well-maintained cars retain value
  • Business opportunities - Potential for ride-sharing services
  • Convenient transportation - Independence from public transport
  • Wide selection - Variety of models available on platforms like Jiji

Disadvantages

  • High maintenance costs - Spare parts can be expensive
  • Fuel expenses - Rising fuel prices affect ownership costs
  • Traffic congestion - Major cities experience significant traffic
  • Import restrictions - Limitations on vehicle age and specifications
